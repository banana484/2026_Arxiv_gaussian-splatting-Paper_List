## Abstracts

<h3 id="paper-1">1. RecallGS: Geometry-Grounded Recall for Online Semantic Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Online semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables open-vocabulary scene understanding from streaming observations. However, existing methods recover geometric context missing from image-derived semantic features using an additionally learned 3D network, incurring extra training and runtime overhead. We observe that such geometric-semantic evidence may already exist in the agent's observation history and can be retrieved rather than learned. Based on this insight, we introduce RecallGS (Geometry-Grounded Recall for Online Semantic Gaussian Splatting), a lightweight, training-free mechanism that treats past observations as a non-parametric geometric-semantic prior. RecallGS follows a recall-ground-fuse pipeline: CLIP retrieves relevant observations, alpha-compositing weights identify geometrically supported views, and aggregate their semantic features over each Gaussian's rendered footprint. On ScanNet and ScanNet++, RecallGS achieves strong semantic segmentation performance at 2.48 FPS, demonstrating that geometric-semantic context can be effectively recovered from observation history without an additional learned 3D branch.

<h3 id="paper-2">2. H-2DGS: Height-Field Gaussian Splatting for Sharp and Deformable Neurosurgical Surface Reconstruction</h3>

Neurosurgical navigation commonly presents magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) as 2D slices, making it difficult to relate the anatomy in those slices to the cortical surface observed through the surgical microscope. We seek a common 3D visualization that combines a photorealistic reconstruction of the exposed cortex during tumor surgery with whole-brain MRI context and diffusion tractography pathways. This requires the reconstructed cortex to form a clear boundary around the brain anatomy rather than a diffuse volume that obscures internal structures. Conventional 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) can reproduce observed views but, under limited surgical view coverage, may distribute opacity through a thick and geometrically ambiguous band. We introduce height-field 2D Gaussian Splatting (H-2DGS), a three-stage pipeline that concentrates a robust 3DGS reconstruction, fuses its depth into a shared truncated signed distance field (TSDF) surface, and represents that surface with curved, surface-oriented Gaussian primitives. Separate height, color, and opacity bases provide local geometry and appearance, while a neighbor-transition objective coordinates overlapping patches. The explicit surface further supports postoperative alignment: MRI-derived depth deforms the reconstruction, and tracked-color supervision with alternating optimization preserves its appearance. Across five real neurosurgical cases, H-2DGS gives low multi-view depth inconsistency, a compact 7.97MB representation, and interactive rendering at 29--42ms per frame. The resulting sharp, deformable surface integrates clearly with tractography and MRI anatomy, including a postoperative resection cavity.

<h3 id="paper-3">3. R4DGS: Referring Segmentation in 4D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Dynamic representations based on 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) have shown strong capabilities in reconstruction and rendering, yet current methods remain limited in supporting complex natural-language referring in dynamic scenes. Existing 4D Gaussian methods are primarily designed for rendering-oriented dynamic modeling, while current semantic extensions often rely on scene-specific semantic optimization or are evaluated on relatively simple query settings. In this paper, we study Referring 4D Gaussian Splatting (R4DGS), a task for grounding temporally varying, multi-target or reasoning-intensive, and zero-target or distractor expressions in dynamic 4D scenes. To support this task, we introduce R4D-Bench-QA, a benchmark with structured query annotations. We further present ReferGaussian, a unified framework built upon 4DGS that converts low-level dynamic Gaussians into persistent, language-addressable entities for training-free referring inference. ReferGaussian organizes mask-supported Gaussian entities in an EntityBank, uses Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to decompose complex queries into trackable object phrases and structured referring constraints, and performs constraint-aware grounding without scene-specific semantic optimization. Experiments on R4D-Bench-QA and the 4D LangSplat protocol demonstrate improved temporal target selection and competitive spatiotemporal grounding without scene-specific semantic training. Project resources are available at https://chenbangpu.github.io/R4DGS/.

<h3 id="paper-4">4. ClunoGS: Compact Language 3D Gaussian Splatting via Unified Optimization</h3>

Language 3D Gaussian Splatting enables open-vocabulary scene understanding by associating Gaussian primitives with language features. These semantic attributes increase model storage, while most existing 3DGS compression methods are designed mainly to preserve RGB rendering quality. One straightforward approach is to apply sparsification and quantization sequentially. However, pruning based only on rendering quality can remove Gaussians that have small RGB contributions but are important for semantic prediction. Once removed, their semantic information cannot be recovered by later quantization. We propose ClunoGS, a compact framework for Language 3DGS that combines semantics-aware Gaussian sparsification and vector quantization in a unified constrained objective. Semantic supervision is used during sparsification, and the objective jointly considers rendering quality, semantic accuracy, and compactness. We optimize the unified objective with alternating primal, sparsification, quantization, and dual updates. We evaluate ClunoGS on open-vocabulary localization and segmentation using the LERF and ScanNet-10 benchmarks. Under aggressive compression, our unified, semantics-aware formulation substantially alleviates the degradation of semantic representations caused by sequential compression and semantics-agnostic Gaussian sparsification. Ablations further show that jointly coordinating sparsification and quantization better preserves semantic accuracy than applying them independently.

<h3 id="paper-5">5. AOV-GS: Active Open-Vocabulary Semantic Mapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Robots deployed in human-centered environments need maps that support both geometric navigation and open-vocabulary semantic queries beyond a fixed label set. A key challenge is enriching such maps with semantic information without relying on manually annotated segmentation masks or closed-set semantic categories. Existing geometry-driven active mapping maximizes coverage but treats semantics as a passive by-product of the collected trajectory, whereas active semantic mapping typically depends on supervised semantic segmentation. We present AOV-GS, an active 3D Gaussian mapping system that guides exploration using frozen foundation models. During coarse exploration, AOV-GS re-ranks geometrically promising candidate views according to CLIP mask-embedding novelty computed from SAM instance segmentation, biasing exploration toward semantically informative regions. Frozen SAM and CLIP provide instance masks and language-aligned embeddings throughout mapping, while a lightweight scene-specific language field is fitted offline directly to the collected CLIP embeddings rather than manually annotated masks, enabling open-vocabulary text-query segmentation on novel views. In a controlled comparison using the same SAM-CLIP perception stack, AOV-GS improves average language-field mIoU over geometry-only active planning by 1.3 percentage points on Replica (34.6% on eight scenes) and by 1.8 points on Matterport3D (23.1% on five scenes). With CorrCLIP, AOV-GS reaches 35.6% on Replica, 2.0 points above the matched Geometry-based + CorrCLIP baseline. Novel-view PSNR remains comparable to the geometry-only baseline on both datasets while depth L1 improves on Replica. Code is available at https://github.com/OptimusFaber/AOV-GS.

<h3 id="paper-6">6. KoalaGS: Complexity-Aware Continuous Level of Detail for Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Deploying a trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene under shrinking compute budgets requires one primitive set to remain effective when rendered at smaller prefixes. In this continuous level of detail (LoD) setting, the challenge is to decide which Gaussians should retain capacity as the budget decreases. A pilot study on 3DGS-MCMC reconstructions shows that image complexity, computed from the input views before optimization, predicts where reconstruction error concentrates in image and 3D space. It also reveals that simple regions contain too many Gaussians and degrade as much as complex ones at lower budgets, despite being inherently cheaper to represent. Subsequently, in KoalaGS, during training we use this complexity prior to bias relocation toward poorly reconstructed areas while ensuring smooth degradation across LoDs. A primitive importance score then enables inference at low-capacity, while preserving the scene structure. Tested across three datasets, KoalaGS maintains competitive full-capacity quality while significantly improving quality across the budget spectrum, yielding smoother transitions across LoDs, and enabling faster progressive streaming.

<h3 id="paper-7">7. Learning from Automatic 3D Gaussian Splatting Edits for Online Bayesian Scene Change Detection</h3>

Online scene change detection requires integrating incoming observations to determine which regions have changed relative to a reference scene. We update change beliefs as new images arrive using generalised Bayesian evidence fusion. However, the prior probability of change and evidence models are unknown beforehand, and numerical Bayesian inference can be costly. We address these challenges by learning offline from automatic edits to a reference 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model. These edits generate labelled training views without manual annotations. Together with unchanged reference observations, they provide supervision for learning scene-specific evidence models and estimating the effective pixel prior and response statistics. Online prediction combines current evidence with historical support, while closed-form local Bayes factors, parameterized by the learned response statistics, update an accumulated 3D Gaussian field. Object-guided refinement produces the final change masks. Our method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on PASLCD and CCS3D, with $67.64\%$ and $75.82\%$ mIoU, respectively. On PASLCD, the complete pipeline runs at $17.2$\,FPS over complete query sequences, the fastest among the evaluated online methods. Project page: https://changruizhu96.github.io/work/lego-scd/

<h3 id="paper-8">8. CrossFit-GS: Out-of-Fold Pose Estimation for COLMAP-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Pose-free 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) aims to bypass cumbersome Structure-from-Motion (SfM) by directly reconstructing scenes from unposed image sequences. In this work, we present CrossFit-GS, a deferred-commitment progressive framework that mitigates cumulative trajectory drift and breaks the self-reinforcing loop of distorted-map error propagation. Specifically, we partition the input sequence into interleaved subsets and maintain two complementary submaps. Each candidate pose is cross-refined against a frozen opposite submap that has never been optimized using the target frame, thereby providing an independent geometric reference for pose estimation. Checkerboard validation and spatial coverage safeguards further reject unreliable updates, and only verified poses are committed to their corresponding owner submap. Once the trajectory has been refined, the intermediate submaps are discarded and the final global representation is trained from scratch using the fixed refined poses. Extensive evaluations across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that CrossFit-GS substantially reduces cumulative trajectory drift and consistently improves both camera pose estimation and novel-view synthesis over existing methods.

<h3 id="paper-9">9. RLSplat: Resource-Linear Budget Control for Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (FF3DGS) reconstructs 3D scenes from images in a single forward pass, making it well-suited to applications that must operate within limited resource budgets. To further improve its practicality, recent budget-controllable FF3DGS methods allow the number of Gaussians to be adjusted at inference, so that the output meets a given storage limit or rendering-latency constraint. However, these methods overlook the cost of inference itself: to produce a representation with the target number of Gaussians, they first predict a dense set of Gaussians and then prune or select from it, requiring nearly constant latency and peak memory regardless of the budget. We therefore propose RLSplat, a resource-linear budget-controllable FF3DGS framework whose representation size, inference latency, and peak memory all decrease linearly as the Gaussian budget is reduced. Instead of pruning or selection, RLSplat learns a Gaussian allocation policy that distributes the budget across local scene regions, so that exactly the requested number of Gaussians is generated. These Gaussians are then refined through resource-linear Gaussian decoding, in which each Gaussian attends only to nearby Gaussians found via an efficient hierarchical neighbor search, so that the decoding cost decreases linearly with fewer Gaussians. In zero-shot evaluation, RLSplat outperforms prior budget-controllable methods at every budget, surpassing the strongest baseline even with 4$\times$ fewer Gaussians, while running up to 4.1$\times$ faster with 9.6$\times$ less peak memory at low budgets.

<h3 id="paper-10">10. PanoGraphGS: Unifying Panoptic Segmentation and Hierarchical 3D Scene Graphs with Gaussian Splatting</h3>

3D scene graphs support language-guided navigation by organizing physical entities and their containment and spatial relations into hierarchical maps. Building such structured maps requires a scene representation that consistently distinguishes semantic regions and object instances across viewpoints. To this end, we present PanoGraphGS, a unified framework that integrates panoptic 3D Gaussian representation learning with hierarchical 3D scene graph construction. The framework learns semantic and instance features on shared Gaussian geometry. Prototype-based semantic learning and association-based instance learning produce consistent panoptic predictions across viewpoints. The learned representation supports novel-view panoptic segmentation and direct Gaussian-level panoptic decoding. A semantic-aware scene graph module further recovers a floor-room-stuff-object hierarchy with containment and spatial relations. We further release NaviGS, a real-world dataset for panoramic scene understanding. The dataset provides 3DGS, mesh, RGB, semantic, and depth data. For 3D scene graph identification, PanoGraphGS outperforms the strongest evaluated baseline in object-node accuracy by more than 50\% points on average across Replica and NaviGS. PanoGraphGS also achieves state-of-the-art performance among the evaluated methods in 2D panoptic segmentation. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of PanoGraphGS in both hierarchical 3D scene understanding and 2D panoptic segmentation. The project website is available at: https://github.com/gaojh0333/PanoGraphGS.

<h3 id="paper-11">11. Counting Contributions, Not Views: Occlusion-Aware Densification for 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

3D Gaussian Splatting grows a dense scene from a sparse initialization through adaptive density control (ADC), driven by per-Gaussian statistics gathered during rendering. These statistics pass a frustum-based visibility filter blind to occlusion: fully occluded Gaussians still count as observed, diluting the averaged gradient signal and suppressing densification precisely where geometry is occluded. We correct this with an occlusion-aware filter that restricts densification statistics to views in which a Gaussian actually contributes, and replace the criterion's raw view-count normalizer with an average-contribution one. Integrated into a StopThePop-based framework, the two together improve every image-quality metric over the occlusion-blind baseline on occlusion-heavy scenes (up to 6% lower LPIPS), at less than half the Gaussians a view-count normalizer requires. Under the longer schedules these large scenes require, the count stays bounded where most criteria diverge, and on smaller benchmarks our method stays competitive at a fraction of the competitors' count.

<h3 id="paper-12">12. SplatXR: A Toolkit for Multi-Layered Extended Reality via On-Headset 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Recent advances in Gaussian splatting, 3D foundation models, and video-passthrough mixed reality offer unprecedented fidelity for capturing and blending physical and virtual worlds. However, established XR methods rely on polygon-based pipelines that treat physical and digital environments as separate domains, making seamless integration across recent novel representations difficult. Because splats lack explicit surface geometry, they impede standard graphics techniques necessary for cross-reality interactions, such as shadow or collision handling. Furthermore, while 3D foundation models can reconstruct physical objects on-the-fly into digital splats, current XR systems fall short of leveraging this capability for user experiences that transition seamlessly between physicality and virtuality. To address this, we organize XR as a multi-layered reality of interacting primitives, letting pixels and objects move across physical and digital boundaries. We contribute SplatXR, a Unity toolkit that operationalizes this structure as building blocks for interactive, immersive apps leveraging 3D Gaussian splatting: a standalone renderer, splat-to-mesh reconstruction, splat physics, splat blending, and offloaded splat object reconstruction via on-headset capture. SplatXR unites vision and graphics techniques to make splat-based XR development broadly available, and we validate it through demonstrative applications and a small developer study.

<h3 id="paper-13">13. Rethinking Sparse-View 3D Gaussian Splatting: A Few-Shot Learning Perspective on Overfitting Prevention</h3>

Sparse-view novel view synthesis (NVS) is essential for AR/VR and real-time communication applications, yet remains challenging when reconstructing 3D scenes from limited viewpoints. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excels in dense-view scenarios, it suffers from overfitting and geometry degradation under sparse inputs. Current approaches often tackle these problems from a local perspective, addressing either data quality or training regularization individually, lacking a unified and interpretable framework for systematic improvement. We introduce a new perspective that reframes sparse-view 3DGS from a few-shot learning viewpoint, treating each Gaussian primitive as a neuron and leveraging deep learning regularization techniques. Under this unified framework, our approach generates more effective supervision from scarce observations through two strategies: (1) data-related supervision enrichment via optical flow-guided pseudo-view synthesis and joint camera-scene optimization; and (2) training-time regularization via motion-aware dropout and early stopping, which suppress overfitting while preserving dynamic-scene stability. Extensive experiments on the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 volumetric video challenge sparse-view dataset demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art optimization-based and feed-forward baselines. Our system won first place in the sparse-view track of the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 challenge.

<h3 id="paper-14">14. MCSplat: Multi-View Photometric and Geometric Consistent Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting for Driving Scenes</h3>

Driving-scene reconstruction via feed-forward Gaussian splatting from sparse multi-view images remains challenging due to two factors. First, cross-camera photometric inconsistency, caused by variations in exposure, ISP, and illumination, can be incorrectly absorbed into Gaussian geometry. Second, unreliable local geometric supervision arises from normal-based constraints near depth discontinuities. To address these issues, we present \textbf{MCSplat}, a multi-view consistent feed-forward Gaussian splatting framework for driving scenes. MCSplat constructs a canonical appearance space from depth-guided multi-camera correspondences to decouple geometry learning from camera-specific appearance. A multi-scale Bilateral Grid Transformer restores raw camera appearance from canonical renderings, enabling photometric supervision without contaminating Gaussian geometry. To improve local geometric reliability, we estimate Fisher-information-based uncertainty from photometric sensitivity and use it to adaptively weight surface-oriented self-supervision. Extensive experiments demonstrate improved reconstruction quality and generalization across driving datasets. Geometry-oriented evaluations further reveal enhanced cross-view depth consistency and local surface-normal agreement, while qualitative results show fewer geometric artifacts and more coherent structures across overlapping views.

<h3 id="paper-15">15. RoMo-GS: Efficient Online 3D Gaussian Splatting for Robot Mapping via Exposure-Integrated Motion Blur</h3>

Online 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is emerging as a practical way to build a representation for robotics applications. However, deploying it on real-world platforms exposes multiple challenges. Rapid maneuvers of a robot induce motion-blurred images, which corrupt the reconstructed geometry and cause camera pose drift. Furthermore, the number of Gaussians grows without bound during continuous online mapping. In this study, we present RoMo-GS, an online 3DGS system designed to address these challenges. Instead of averaging N discrete sub-frames as in prior motion blur-aware 3DGS, we analytically integrate each projected Gaussian over the exposure interval, yielding an O(1) complexity per Gaussian. Building on this, we bound the Gaussians' growth with a Hessian-based pruning score, while jointly refining pose, velocity, and Gaussians with Adam and an online LM step over the Gaussians. On multiple public datasets and a self-collected dataset, RoMo-GS matches or exceeds offline deblurring 3DGS methods in rendering quality while running 15--150x faster, and operates online at competitive quality with about 20% fewer Gaussians than the online 3DGS baseline.

<h3 id="paper-16">16. Codec-Compatible Learned Compression for 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Video-based 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) compression methods provide a practical route by representing Gaussian attributes as 2D maps and coding them with standard video codecs. Among these maps, high-dimensional non-DC spherical harmonics (SH-AC) maps contain the largest number of channels and account for a dominant portion of the bitstream. However, their statistics differ substantially from those of natural images, and the nonlinear dependencies among SH-AC coefficients remain insufficiently exploited. To address these issues, we propose CCT-GS, a Codec-Compatible learned Transform framework for 3DGS compression. CCT-GS first learns a generalized SH-AC transform that captures cross-scene regularities and converts SH-AC maps into compact codec-friendly bottleneck maps. It further introduces LoRA-based scene adaptation to recover scene-specific appearance details with lightweight side information. Experiments on MPEG GSC sequences demonstrate that CCT-GS consistently outperforms representative video-based baselines, achieving an average BD-rate reduction of 51.30% over the video-based anchor.

<h3 id="paper-17">17. GTSM: Gaussian Splatting with Thermal State-Space Dynamics for Spatio-Temporal Thermal Field Reconstruction and Prediction</h3>

Temperature-field reconstruction and prediction are important for condition monitoring, process analysis, and state assessment in industrial and dynamic environments. This work aims to recover a four-dimensional spatio-temporal thermal state field directly from two-dimensional observations, without requiring a predefined 3D model, prescribed thermodynamic equation, or predefined heating/cooling trajectory. To this end, we propose the Gaussian Thermal State-Space Model (GTSM), which represents thermal evolution through a shared scene-level latent process and Gaussian-specific local thermal states. The resulting continuous dynamics are propagated using differentiable RK2 integration, allowing thermal evolution to be inferred from multi-view and multi-temporal observations and continuously extrapolated beyond the observed interval. GTSM is designed as a backbone-agnostic thermal dynamics module for Gaussian representations and can be integrated with both static 3D Gaussian Splatting and dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting, supporting thermal reconstruction and prediction under both static and time-varying target geometry. The reconstructed representation further enables novel-view thermal rendering and future-time thermal-state prediction. Experiments on real infrared sequences and synthetic dynamic thermal data demonstrate improved reconstruction accuracy and substantially stronger temporal extrapolation than representative baselines, while also validating the applicability of GTSM to deforming targets.

<h3 id="paper-18">18. X-Splat: Unified Gaussian Splatting for Joint RGB and X-ray 3D Reconstruction</h3>

Jointly reconstructing RGB and X-ray provides new insights by connecting external appearance with internal structure, enabling a more complete understanding of real-world objects than single modality. Yet existing methods train separate models per modality and suffer from redundant geometry, alignment artifacts, and loose coupling that limits cross-modal insight discovery. We propose a unified representation in which all Gaussian primitives share a single set of geometric parameters, with only surface-proximal primitives additionally carrying appearance attributes. We perform cross-modal registration through clean multi-view stereo and X-ray geometric proxies instead of aligning two sets of optimized Gaussians, improving alignment stability. We then jointly optimize the unified representation via supervision from both modalities, complemented by a surface-depth consistency loss derived from X-ray geometry to guide RGB reconstruction. Experiments on the InsideOut dataset and our Flower & Insect dataset show that our framework achieves more robust cross-modal alignment, higher RGB rendering quality without compromising X-ray reconstruction, and a more compact representation than maintaining two independent geometries.

<h3 id="paper-19">19. Frame-Consistent Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting from Sparse Unposed Images</h3>

Feed-forward Gaussian models have made novel view synthesis almost instantaneous. Feed-forward methods predict Gaussians directly, either in two stages or in a single merged pass. Using two separately trained models is attractive because each can specialize in its own objective: one for accurate pose estimation and the other for high-quality reconstruction, rather than forcing a single jointly trained network to compromise between the two. A natural instantiation pairs a frozen pose estimator, VGGT, with a frozen reconstruction backbone, TokenGS. Prior work, such as VGGT-X and 3R-GS, also adopts a two-stage design, but attributes the resulting quality degradation to noisy pose estimates and introduces learned refinement. We show that this is not the primary source of the problem. Through per-camera analysis across 107 scenes from two datasets, DL3DV and MipNeRF360, using COLMAP poses as the reference, we find that the rotation error after alignment is small (median 1.6 degrees), whereas the translation scale is offset by a large, dataset-consistent factor. Controlled ablations over 140 DL3DV scenes show that the primary obstacles are two integration failures rather than pose error: a coordinate-frame inconsistency and a translation-scale mismatch, both of which can be corrected deterministically. With these fixes, a frozen VGGT driving a frozen TokenGS achieves 22.47 PSNR, within 0.51 dB of the COLMAP reference, with no additional training, when poses are estimated from all scene views. In the sparse setting, where reconstruction uses only four context images under the standard pose-free protocol, the same pipeline matches this all-view quality, yielding a fully COLMAP-free pipeline without any refinement step.

<h3 id="paper-20">20. KFS$^4$LAM: Adaptive KeyFrame Selection with Structure-Aware Mapping for Endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM</h3>

Gaussian-splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) augments conventional SLAM with a dense, renderable 3D scene representation, enabling high-quality reconstruction and novel-view synthesis. However, its localization, reconstruction, and rendering performance depends strongly on which observations are retained for mapping. Existing systems commonly rely on fixed temporal intervals or simple pose- and overlap-based criteria for keyframe selection. In colonoscopy, non-uniform camera motion, repetitive anatomy, specularities, and visual artifacts can make these criteria unreliable, leading to either redundant keyframes or missed informative viewpoints. We present KFS$^4$LAM (KeyFrame Selection with Structure-aware mapping for endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM), which couples motion-guided adaptive keyframe selection with structurally supervised mapping and restricted temporal deformation. Accumulated motion guides adaptive keyframe selection, with complementary cues suppressing redundant or unreliable candidates. Density-aware mapping with anatomical contour supervision (colon folds) emphasizes structurally informative regions, while restricted temporal deformation accommodates local tissue dynamics without unconstrained geometric drift. We evaluate KFS$^4$LAM against Gaussian-SLAM baselines on C3VD and C3VD-v2, together with cross-backbone and component ablations. KFS$^4$LAM achieves strong overall performance across trajectory, rendering, and depth metrics, with pronounced gains on more challenging C3VD-v2 dataset. In the matched comparison with SplaTAM on C3VD-v2, it reduces rotational relative pose error by 20.3% and depth RMSE by 38.4%, while improving PSNR by 0.78 dB. Cross-backbone experiments further show that the framework is not tied to a single underlying SLAM architecture. Code and visualization results are available at https://xinweiju.github.io/KFSLAM/.

<h3 id="paper-21">21. Anchor3DGS: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting with Compact Anchor-Based Representation</h3>

Recent feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods have enabled effective multi-view 3D reconstruction by predicting Gaussian primitives in a single forward pass. However, existing approaches predominantly adopt a pixel-aligned paradigm that predicts one Gaussian per input pixel, coupling the Gaussian count to the input resolution and number of views. This also leads to redundant primitives in textureless regions and insufficient coverage in geometrically complex areas. We present Anchor3DGS, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that replaces pixel-aligned prediction with a compact anchor-based representation. Guided by information entropy, our method places a set of 3D anchors in the scene, concentrating them in regions of higher visual complexity while maintaining broad spatial coverage. Each anchor performs occlusion-aware aggregation of multi-view image features, exchanges information with other anchors, and is decoded into a set of local Gaussians. A lightweight feature enhancement module further refines the coarse RGB output from Gaussian splatting and improves rendering fidelity. Extensive experiments on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that our Anchor3DGS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis quality while only using an order of magnitude fewer Gaussian primitives.

<h3 id="paper-22">22. ResStreamGS: Residual-Sensitive Dual-Stream Gaussian Splatting for Mobile Volumetric Video Streaming</h3>

Real-time mobile volumetric video enables immersive free-viewpoint experiences, but delivering high-quality 3D content under constrained bandwidth and computation remains challenging. Recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) systems, such as V$^3$, enable efficient mobile streaming by encoding time-varying Gaussian attributes with commodity video codecs and hardware decoding. However, existing pipelines treat all Gaussians as frame-varying signals, which is poorly suited to full-scene content where large portions remain temporally stable. Target and non-target evidence are accumulated simultaneously, weighted by the visibility of each Gaussian in each view. After that, the Gaussians are filtered with two thresholds: a main threshold $β$ selects the high-confidence seeds, and a lower one $γβ$ adds the connected components around them. For the evaluation, the labels are transferred from the Gaussians to the mesh vertices that are both visible and annotated. With this design, we can separate three sources of error: the 2D detector, the lifting and the transfer between representations. The thresholds and the transfer operator are chosen on seven Replica validation scenes, and the method is evaluated on ten held-out ScanNet++ scenes with the same values for every scene and class. The mean mIoU on the validation scenes was 0.93 with masks from the dataset annotations and 0.65 with YOLO masks, and on the ScanNet++ test scenes it was 0.80 and 0.54. Compared with thresholding the evidence per view, as a previous version of the method did, the fraction improves the test mIoU by 0.24 and makes it possible to use a single threshold for all the classes and scenes of both datasets. Finally, the error analysis shows that most of the remaining error comes from the detector.

The corresponding affiliated-eye quality degradation is at most 1.3 dB PSNR, 0.02 SSIM, and 0.02 LPIPS, representing a measurable trade-off that requires application-specific perceptual validation. These results establish a practical efficiency-quality trade-off for controlled static-scene stereo rendering and motivate future evaluation under continuous motion and on physical VR hardware.

<h3 id="paper-95">95. LiTe-GS: Oracle-Efficient Next Best View Selection for 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Selecting informative camera views is critical for efficient training and adaptive refinement in 3D Gaussian Splatting, where each observation significantly influences model parameters. However, information-driven view-selection strategies can require repeated evaluations of expensive information-gain oracles as the number of candidate views increases. We propose LiTe-GS, an oracle-efficient method for next best view selection in 3D Gaussian Splatting. LiTe-GS reduces the number of information-oracle evaluations by performing randomized subset evaluation of candidate views rather than exhaustively scoring the full candidate pool. The resulting approach achieves expected $O(M\log(1/ε))$ oracle complexity with respect to the number of candidate views $M$, independent of the selection cardinality $K$, while providing an explicit trade-off between oracle efficiency and approximation quality through $ε$. We provide theoretical guarantees on oracle complexity and approximation performance under the proposed selection scheme. Experiments on Blender and Mip-NeRF 360 demonstrate that LiTe-GS maintains reconstruction quality comparable to Fisher-information-based baselines while substantially reducing the number of Fisher-oracle evaluations across different acquisition settings.

<h3 id="paper-96">96. Towards Practical Compression of 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables high-quality novel-view synthesis but requires substantial storage. Existing compression methods often rely on spatial context modeling over irregular 3D representations, increasing the complexity of training and coding. Meanwhile, floating-point context inference can introduce numerical inconsistencies across platforms, causing entropy-decoding failures. To address these practical challenges, we propose COSA-GS, which constructs context without spatial aggregation through anchor-wise causal factorization. Specifically, we use geometry context derived from each anchor's coordinates to model a compact learnable anchor latent. The anchor latent is then fused with the geometry context to form an anchor context for attribute coding. The resulting context model features a simple architecture composed solely of linear transformations and activations. We train COSA-GS using rate--distortion optimization with adaptive Gaussian pruning. Further, we develop quantization-aware training and integer inference for the context model to achieve bit-exact consistency of entropy-decoded symbols across platforms. Experiments demonstrate that COSA-GS achieves state-of-the-art compression performance while retaining fast and consistent cross-platform decoding, providing a simple yet effective framework for practical 3DGS compression. Code is available at https://github.com/pengpeng-yu/COSA-GS.

<h3 id="paper-97">97. OceanXL: Large-scale Underwater 3D Gaussian Splatting via Block Partitioning and Adaptive Pruning</h3>

Underwater 3D reconstruction is critical for marine exploration, ecological monitoring, and subsea infrastructure inspection, yet remains challenging at large scale due to light attenuation, scattering, and limited capture coverage. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables high-quality real-time rendering, its application to large underwater scenes is constrained by high memory consumption and inefficient optimization over extensive areas. We propose OceanXL, a fast and scalable 3DGS-based framework for large-scale underwater reconstruction. OceanXL adopts a divide-and-conquer strategy, partitioning scenes into spatially coherent blocks to enable efficient optimization while preserving global geometric consistency. We further introduce an adaptive pruning scheme tailored to underwater conditions that removes redundant primitives, producing compact representations without sacrificing visual fidelity. Together, these components improve training efficiency and rendering performance for large scenes. We also introduce a large-scale underwater dataset covering diverse marine environments. Experiments on five large-scale scenes demonstrate favorable scalability, compactness, and efficiency--quality trade-offs over large-scene baselines. Controlled comparisons on the small-scale SeaThru-NeRF dataset further show competitive reconstruction quality with substantially smaller model sizes than underwater-specific methods.

<h3 id="paper-98">98. ADATEX4D: adaptive texture capacity allocation for 4D gaussian splatting</h3>

Textured Gaussians improve local appearance capacity, but assigning the same texture resolution to every primitive wastes storage on low-detail or weakly visible regions. We introduce AdaTex4D, an adaptive texture-capacity module for deformation-based 4D Gaussian Splatting. Each Gaussian carries packed RGBA triplanes whose two axes grow independently according to visibility normalized screen-space gradients and deformed local scales. Experiments on N3DV and PanopticSports show that AdaTex4D reduces texture storage by more than half while preserving reconstruction quality. Under fixed memory budgets, adaptive allocation also improves quality over uniform texture assignment and reduces overall model and peak memory. These results show that dynamic, anisotropic texture allocation provides a more efficient way to distribute local appearance capacity in 4D Gaussian representations.

<h3 id="paper-99">99. SplatLabel: Pseudo-Labelling through 4D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

While 2D Vision Foundation Models offer a pathway to automate 3D semantic pseudo-labelling, translating these priors into robust 3D representations typically requires complex heuristics or multi-model ensembles. We introduce SplatLabel, an automated pipeline that leverages a 4D Gaussian representation to extract LiDAR segmentation with predictive confidence, as well as semantic occupancy grids at arbitrary voxel resolutions. At its core, SplatLabel handles dynamic environments through an explicit temporal manifold that models the trajectories and lifespans of individual 3D primitives. This allows the system to accurately track moving actors and strictly define when objects appear and disappear, completely eliminating the need for pre-annotated 3D bounding boxes. To robustly support this dynamic tracking, the representation is grounded by structural and semantic priors: we guide scene geometry in unobserved regions by integrating 360-degree LiDAR via virtual depth maps, and rather than relying on domain-specific prompt engineering, we directly distill continuous soft probabilities from 2D models to inherently resolve semantic ambiguities over time and space. Finally, to accurately reflect the real-world trade-off between precision and recall, we reframe pseudo-label evaluation as a selective classification task using a generalized risk-recall metric. Experiments on SemanticKITTI demonstrate that SplatLabel consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across multiple recall levels, establishing a highly robust framework for both 3D LiDAR segmentation and occupancy prediction.

<h3 id="paper-100">100. From Scattered Gaussians to Structured Maps: Efficient Gaussian Splatting Coding via Dual-phase Morton Sorting</h3>

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables high fidelity novel view synthesis but suffers from excessive storage and bandwidth requirements due to its unstructured representation. To address this, a projection based video coding framework has emerged as a leading approach, supported by MPEG's ongoing standardization, where 3DGS attributes are converted into 2D maps to take advantage of efficient compression using established video codecs such as HEVC and VVC. However, the effectiveness of this approach depends heavily on the spatial coherence of the projected video, which current sorting strategies such as PLAS and Morton ordering fail to preserve adequately, either incurring high computational cost or achieving limited correlation retention. To overcome these limitations, we propose a dual phase Morton spatial sorting algorithm that improves both coding efficiency and processing speed. In the first phase, Morton based 1D indexing is applied to high dimensional attributes to enhance spatial locality. The second phase further refines layout continuity through a structured 2D Morton mapping table that enforces spatial adjacency. This hierarchical strategy generates highly regular, block wise feature maps with strong local correlation, making them well suited for compression via conventional block based coding tools. Experimental results show that our method significantly outperforms existing approaches in both compression performance and runtime efficiency, providing a practical and standard compatible solution for 3DGS data coding.
