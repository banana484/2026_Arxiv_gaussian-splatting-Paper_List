## Abstracts

<h3 id="paper-1">1. FastWild-GS: Minutes-Scale In-the-Wild Gaussian Splatting via Compact Per-Image Appearance Adaptation</h3>

Internet photos of a scene differ in exposure, illumination and transient objects such as passers-by, so one set of Gaussian colours cannot explain all photos (the in-the-wild setting). Recent in-the-wild Gaussian Splatting methods mostly report hours of per-scene optimization. We present FastWild-GS, which describes each photo by a 128-dimensional Photo Appearance Token and a per-channel transform, a scale and an offset applied to the rendered image (six parameters); a small MLP predicts the colour of each Gaussian from this token and a per-Gaussian feature, replacing the spherical-harmonics colour. The same transform is applied to the renders that score Gaussians for densification and pruning, so appearance differences between photos are not counted as reconstruction error. On PhotoTourism, FastWild-GS trains in 8.8 minutes per scene with the lowest LPIPS among the compared methods, 23x faster than AsymGS, the next best in LPIPS. On NeRF-OSR, it has the best average PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS among the compared methods in 9.7 minutes per scene.

<h3 id="paper-2">2. RF3DGS: Reflection-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting with Self-Priors</h3>

3D reflection removal is crucial in applications demanding high-fidelity visualization, such as museum exhibitions. In this work, we propose Reflection-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting (RF3DGS), a novel method for superior reflection removal. RF3DGS exclusively leverages a widely-adopted photometric prior for reflection removal: that the minimum pixel intensity across multi-view images corresponding to the same 3D point strongly correlates with reflection-free information. However, generalizing this principle to a 3D Gaussian Splatting within a multi-view context is non-trivial. To address this, RF3DGS first optimizes a vanilla 3DGS model using a geometric prior to estimate a coarse, reflection-free depth map for each view. Leveraging these depth maps and photometric prior, a 3D warping process aggregates reflection-free information from auxiliary views to construct a clean transmission pseudo-label for a given reference view. This pseudo-label provides a robust self-supervised signal for optimization. We further introduce an Adaptive Twin Rendering (ATR) loss to synergistically utilize this pseudo-label while preserving high-frequency details from the original, reflection-degraded images. By adeptly fusing these internal 3D self-priors with the ATR loss, RF3DGS demonstrates state-of-the-art reflection removal performance across both synthetic and real-world scenarios. Furthermore, we establish the first 3D reflection removal benchmark, based on the Unity rendering engine, for rigorous quantitative evaluation.

<h3 id="paper-3">3. DepthGS: Depth-Guided Gaussian Splatting with Adaptive Regularization for High-Quality Surface Reconstruction</h3>

Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as an efficient representation for novel view synthesis, offering fast differentiable rendering and high visual fidelity, and has increasingly been extended to surface reconstruction. However, the relationship between depth information and reconstruction error remains insufficiently explored, limiting the principled design of geometric optimization strategies. To fill the above gap, we propose DepthGS, a depth-guided Gaussian splatting framework. We derive theoretical relations between depth discrepancies and reconstruction error under single-view and multi-view settings, and rigorously establish corresponding upper bounds on reconstruction error. The above analysis informs the design of adaptive regularization strategies in both single-view and multi-view optimization. More specifically, for single-view optimization, we propose a depth-difference-guided adaptive normal regularization strategy that modulates the normal constraint strength using a geometric reliability weight computed from local depth differences. For multi-view optimization, we introduce a cross-view depth consistency regularization strategy that promotes consistency between neighboring-view depth maps and the corresponding depth predictions obtained by transforming the current view's depth predictions into those neighboring views. Extensive experiments on DTU, Mip-NeRF360, and Tanks and Temples demonstrate that DepthGS achieves competitive performance in both surface reconstruction and novel view synthesis.

<h3 id="paper-4">4. GEMOR-GS: Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAM</h3>

Photorealistic and metrically consistent world representations are a primary requirement for LiDAR--inertial--visual SLAM systems that serve robots over continuous operation. 3D Gaussian splatting makes such representations attainable by supplying an explicit, renderable, and queryable scene model. We present GEMOR-GS (Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAM) to make the Gaussian map geometrically accurate, globally refined, and re-enterable, providing a better appearance for robot perception and decision-making. To improve the 3DGS map, the LiDAR--camera extrinsic is first estimated offline by direct-method alignment over dedicated calibration segments and reused by all following online and offline processes. Under the calibrated geometry, keyframe poses and Gaussian parameters are refined alternately under a gated differentiable-rendering and LiDAR--projection objective. The final global reconstruction is then rebuilt from the refined keyframe schema with all online constraints lifted. For the robot SLAM lifecycle, the method recovers the pose from the 3DGS map after tracking loss through an XFeat bypass map, RANSAC-PnP coarse localization, and Gaussian RGB-D warp refinement. Experiments on one self-collected and five public sequences quantify each element: the offline reconstruction wins all paired keyframes against the online map by 14.3--19.9 dB, improves PSNR by approximately 9 dB over Gaussian-LIC2, and achieves 86--100% held-out relocalization success tied to extrinsic quality. The results show that GEMOR-GS improves the geometric accuracy, appearance fidelity, and re-enterability of the 3DGS map, serving as a dependable robot world model for SLAM and downstream tasks.

<h3 id="paper-5">5. RetinexGS: Retinex-Inspired Gaussian Splatting for Low-Light RAW Reconstruction</h3>

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pipelines struggle with severely underexposed images, producing unstable colors, floating artifacts, and incomplete reconstructions in dark regions. To better account for noise in these low-light observations, RAW images offer a natural basis for noise-aware reconstruction by preserving linear sensor measurements and providing access to noise metadata. We present RetinexGS, a RAW-domain 3DGS framework that combines Retinex-inspired appearance modeling with sensor-informed optimization for low-light novel-view synthesis. We factorize each Gaussian's color into a view-independent base color and a shared, position- and view-dependent modulation field, regularizing the two components separately to constrain appearance learning from noisy observations. We use DNG noise metadata to weight the reconstruction loss for signal-dependent noise, while distortion-aware rendering avoids spatially resampling RAW targets. To address sparse geometry in dark regions, we introduce depth-guided initialization. We evaluate RetinexGS on three public RawNeRF scenes and two custom-captured low-light RAW scenes. With all methods trained on the same RAW inputs for each scene, RetinexGS outperforms LE3D and RawNeRF in mean sRGB PSNR by 2.18 dB and 2.71 dB respectively, across the five scenes, while supporting real-time novel-view rendering.

<h3 id="paper-6">6. Plant Orbiter: 3D Plant Reconstruction and Generation with Video Diffusion Orbit Cameras and 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Modern digital phenotyping pipelines require the precise measurement of a range of 3D morphological plant traits. The accurate, high throughput acquisition of these traits helps breeders and researchers accelerate the development of new varieties, techniques and products. Recent advances in 3D technologies have enabled a rapid transition from 2D to 3D pipelines, but high throughput acquisition of digital 3D twins of plants remains a significant challenge due to complex morphology, thin structures and occlusions. In this paper we present Plant Orbiter, a new approach to generating 3D plants that leverages recent advances in video diffusion to produce high quality 3D Gaussian Splats of plants from a single image. We show that our method more accurately recreates plant morphology than other state-of-the-art methods, achieving the highest 3D F-score against all baselines, while being more practical for high throughput pipelines due to easier alignment and reduced cost of camera placement. Combined with a 2D diffusion model, we show that our method also achieves state-of-the-art results in the generation of 3D plant models from a text prompt.

<h3 id="paper-7">7. Mitigating SM-Level Load Imbalance in 3D Gaussian Splatting on GPUs</h3>

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables photorealistic, real-time rendering on commodity GPUs and is increasingly adopted in generative world models and autonomous driving simulators. However, existing optimizations rely on algorithmic changes that often require retraining, or on specialized accelerators that lack flexibility across evolving 3DGS variants. In this paper, we identify a fundamental inefficiency in GPU-based 3DGS rendering: severe SM-level load imbalance caused by uneven workloads across screen tiles. This imbalance creates a long execution tail, leaving most SMs idle while a few process computationally intensive tiles, particularly in large-scale urban scenes. To address this, we present a software-defined GPU scheduling approach that mitigates the execution tail. Our approach uses a persistent kernel to schedule heavy tiles first and selectively chunks outlier tiles across multiple CTAs. A lightweight per-frame policy determines when and how much to chunk using tile statistics already available in the rendering pipeline. Our approach requires neither retraining nor specialized hardware and can be applied orthogonally to existing optimizations across diverse 3DGS variants. Averaged per scene, it accelerates alpha blending by up to 2.93x on a large-scale urban scene, and by up to 5.6x on its most imbalanced frames, while retaining the original alpha blending formulation.

<h3 id="paper-8">8. DPGS-SLAM: Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM for Dynamic Visual Sensing</h3>

This paper presents DPGS-SLAM (Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM) for dense mapping in environments containing dynamic objects. Dynamic observations can contaminate camera tracking and scene reconstruction in visual SLAM (simultaneous localization and mapping). Although 3DGS (3D Gaussian splatting) enables efficient dense mapping, existing 3DGS-SLAM systems may exhibit instability when dynamic objects violate their underlying static-scene assumption. Excluding dynamic observations can reduce their influence on tracking and mapping, but may leave occluded static backgrounds missing or weakly constrained. Our proposed DPGS-SLAM is a monocular RGB SLAM framework that supports provisional representations of insufficiently observed static backgrounds through asynchronous diffusion-based background completion and provenance-aware Gaussian mapping. Dynamic regions are identified through optical-flow-based motion discovery and temporally consistent mask propagation by SAM2 (segment anything model 2). For selected keyframes, a fine-tuned diffusion inpainting model generates plausible background appearance in occluded regions, while Metric3D v2 predicts metric depth from the completed images to initialize provisional Gaussian primitives. Provenance labels distinguish generated primitives from observation-supported primitives, excluding generated content from camera-pose estimation and observed-map optimization. When previously occluded backgrounds become visible, eligible generated primitives are removed, and observation-supported primitives are independently initialized from real RGB observations and predicted depth. Experimental results demonstrated competitive camera tracking, with average ATE RMSE values of 1.2 cm on TUM RGB-D and 2.7 cm on BONN RGB-D. On StaDy4D, DPGS-SLAM improved average PSNR by 4.60 dB over WildGS-SLAM using only monocular RGB input.

<h3 id="paper-9">9. UltraVoxelGS: Voxel-First Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting for 3D Ultrasound Reconstruction</h3>

We present UltraVoxelGS, a 3D ultrasound reconstruction method that achieves both fast reconstruction and physically faithful rendering for the first time, bringing live spatial feedback during 2D ultrasound scanning within reach. This is made possible by a feed-forward design that predicts a complete Gaussian scene representation from posed ultrasound images directly, entirely bypassing the per-scene optimization bottleneck that has confined prior methods to offline use. Realising such feed-forward prediction in this setting is non-trivial: unlike natural images, ultrasound slices share at most one-dimensional intersections and exhibit view-dependent appearance due to acoustic propagation and attenuation, violating the dense overlap and photometric consistency assumed by existing feed-forward Gaussian methods. Hence, to achieve fast reconstruction, we introduce a feed-forward voxel-to-Gaussian pass that decouples representation size from input count by lifting all posed slices into a fixed-size voxel support and predicting Gaussian parameters directly. For faithful rendering, we propose ultrasound-aware appearance rendering strategy factorize reflection from cumulative attenuation during training and exploit appearance continuity among spatially adjacent slices for efficient Render-time Appearance Adaptation. On both real-world and simulated datasets, UltraVoxelGS achieves best PSNR/SSIM compared with per-scene optimization baselines while reducing inference from 10-20 minutes to approximately 10 seconds (over 35 times faster) and scales to sequences exceeding 1,000 frames. Code is available at https://github.com/YuebyYue/UltraVoxelGS-camera-ready.

<h3 id="paper-10">10. PLS-GS: Probabilistic Lidar Scaffolds for Compact, Surface-Aligned Gaussian Splatting</h3>

While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is typically optimized from images only, many robotics and mapping applications provide both camera and high-definition LiDAR data, combining rich visual appearance with accurate geometric structure. We introduce PLS-GS, a probabilistic LiDAR scaffold for Gaussian Splatting that exploits this complementary sensing setting to construct a compact 3DGS representation aligned to the measured surface. PLS-GS fits a Hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Model jointly over position and color, yielding a continuous and adaptive scaffold that eliminates the need for discrete scene partitioning such as voxel grids or octrees. The fitted mixture then guides the renderable 3DGS Gaussians: each rendering Gaussian is associated with a GMM component and constrained in the local surface frame induced by that component's covariance. A Mahalanobis support loss bounds the deviation of the rendering Gaussians from the corresponding LiDAR-derived surface. Across five large outdoor scenes, PLS-GS places primitives markedly closer to the LiDAR measurements than both image- and LiDAR-initialized baselines, while employing substantially fewer primitives, yielding a considerably more compact model and faster rendering. At matched capacity, PLS-GS places primitives closer to the LiDAR measurements, with comparable rendering quality. Code is available at https://github.com/k-harikumar/PLS-GS.

<h3 id="paper-11">11. SHIN: Scalable Hierarchy for INcremental 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides high-quality real-time novel-view synthesis, but deployment budgets vary across devices and network conditions. Most compact methods target one operating point, so another budget requires a separate representation. SHIN is a scalable 3DGS hierarchy with a reusable Base and additive enhancement layers, so each higher-quality operating point extends rather than replaces the decoded representation. Constructing this hierarchy requires deciding which Gaussians each stage adds and how much spherical-harmonic (SH) appearance each retains, because these choices remain fixed in later prefixes. SHIN derives one shared Gaussian order from multi-scale position-scale sensitivity and divides each stage budget between adding Gaussians and retaining SH bands using calibrated gradient-based scores per byte. Lightweight opacity updates adjust compositing while transmitted geometry and SH coefficients remain unchanged. Across three benchmarks, SHIN progressively improves one cumulative representation and achieves the best PSNR-size efficiency in every overlapping comparison with the multi-rate and progressive baselines.

<h3 id="paper-12">12. RefineSplat: Reconstruction-Guided Object-Centric Gaussian Splatting for Open-Vocabulary Querying</h3>

Open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding aims to segment and retrieve arbitrary objects in 3D scenes using natural language queries. Most existing methods typically follow a splat-then-segment paradigm, where 3D Gaussian Splatting is first reconstructed and then augmented with labels or language features for subsequent segmentation. However, this pipeline often suffers from view inconsistency, ambiguous object boundaries, and weak object-level correspondence, especially in complex static and dynamic scenes. Building on our prior Segment-then-Splat framework, we present RefineSplat, a reconstruction-guided extension that retains its object-centric paradigm: it first tracks objects in 2D and then reconstructs each object with a dedicated set of 3D Gaussians, preserving explicit object-Gaussian correspondence for unified open-vocabulary segmentation and retrieval. To improve robustness, we further close the loop between 2D tracking and 3D reconstruction. Since off-the-shelf video trackers can produce unreliable masks that propagate errors into object reconstruction, we introduce a reconstruction-guided feedback mechanism that refines object tracking using rendered masks and multi-view consistency. Moreover, instead of assigning each object a single language embedding, we propose a viewpoint-aware embedding assignment strategy, where each object maintains multiple view-clustered embeddings to capture view-dependent semantic cues. We also introduce Blender-OVS, a synthetic benchmark of static and dynamic sequences with exact object meshes and point clouds, enabling direct evaluation of extracted geometry. Experiments on static and dynamic benchmarks show improved rendered-view segmentation and lower object-level Chamfer distances, with competitive scene reconstruction quality.

<h3 id="paper-13">13. ClearForm: Shared-Surface Gaussian Splatting for Transparent and Opaque Scenes</h3>

Transparent-interface reconstruction must recover glass surfaces even when most observed color comes from the scene behind them. We present ClearForm, a shared-planar-Gaussian method for jointly reconstructing transparent interfaces and the appearance seen through them. We identify and analyze the Geometry-Appearance Compositing Dilemma (GACD): when the same transparency-dependent alpha controls both color and geometry, revealing the background can weaken a transparent surface's contribution to geometric reconstruction. We derive its effect on plane-based depth estimation, identify a mechanism that can weaken geometric learning, and quantify the loss of interface support through controlled comparisons. To address this conflict, we develop geometry-preserving dual-opacity surface reconstruction. Shared planar Gaussians retain interface contributions in geometry compositing while modeling transmission and sparse reflection in RGB, supported by transparency-aware geometric constraints. To manage the different primitive-visibility requirements of these compositors, we introduce Dual-Evidence Lifecycle (DEL), which retains Gaussians supported by multi-view geometry or RGB reconstruction evidence. On 3D-FRONT-T, ClearForm achieves 27.05 mm Chamfer distance (CD) and 0.928 F1, compared with 59.18 mm CD for our PGSR reproduction and the reported 34 mm CD and 0.836 F1 for GLINT. On TransLab, it achieves 1.65 mm CD, 0.963 F1, and 40.14 dB PSNR. In matched ablations, dual-opacity compositing reduces CD from 59.25 to 27.09 mm, while DEL improves PSNR by 2.06 dB. Our project page is available at https://hajeongx.github.io/clearform/.

<h3 id="paper-14">14. RigidGS: Geometry-Aware Object Decoupling for Rigid-Body Interaction in 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Interactive VR/MR applications demand photorealistic 3D representations that support real-time rendering and object-level manipulation. <h3 id="paper-15">15. InstantHand: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting for Animatable Hand Avatar Reconstruction from a Single Image</h3>

<h3 id="paper-16">16. From Video to Digital Twin: An Exploratory Study of World Models and Gaussian Splatting on In-the-Wild Video</h3>

Generative world models and 3D Gaussian Splatting promise to turn ordinary video into explorable 3D scenes, but little is reported on how they behave on uncurated, in-the-wild footage. <h3 id="paper-17">17. BudgetSplat: Budget-Controllable Generalizable Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting enables efficient generalizable novel view synthesis, but existing pixel-aligned methods often generate highly redundant Gaussian primitives, causing the final representation size and rendering cost to grow rapidly with image resolution and the number of input views. We propose BudgetSplat, a budget-controllable feed-forward framework that adapts the final Gaussian representation to a user-specified preservation ratio. Rather than treating compaction as simple importance ranking, we formulate it as a joint problem of importance estimation, spatial coverage, and cross-view redundancy. A single trained model supports varying numbers of context views and multiple test-time budgets without per-budget retraining or per-scene compression. To realize this formulation, Intra-Cross Mask Supervision provides structure-, coverage-, and redundancy-aware guidance for learning Gaussian importance, while budget-conditioned feature modulation adapts Gaussian opacity and attributes to the requested preservation ratio. Coverage-Aware Budget Selection further combines patch-wise pre-selection, view-wise opacity ranking, and 3D redundancy suppression to construct compact yet spatially distributed Gaussian representations. Experiments on indoor reconstruction and cross-dataset generalization demonstrate consistent quality--size trade-offs across different input view counts and preservation ratios, substantially reducing final representation storage and rendering cost while preserving rendering quality.

<h3 id="paper-18">18. CrossPressor: Scalable Cross-View Fusion for Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) becomes increasingly difficult to scale to long multi-view sequences. Anchor-based compression methods such as ZPressor alleviate representation growth by decoding Gaussians only from a compact set of anchor feature maps, but keeping these anchors fixed forces each one to aggregate an increasing number of support views. We identify this representation--fusion mismatch and introduce \textbf{CrossPressor}, a training-free method for scalable anchor-based fusion. CrossPressor preserves all decoder-facing anchor queries while constraining the view-growing support sequence through coverage-cycled evidence routing, distributing retained spatial evidence across support views and fusion layers while keeping all support views represented. Routing before K/V projection, together with joint context packing, further reduces support-side fusion cost without retraining. CrossPressor maintains reconstruction quality close to dense fusion while achieving up to $26.12\times$ faster cross-view fusion and $1.43\times$ end-to-end acceleration on long input sequences. <h3 id="paper-19">19. METRIC-GS: Selective Metric-Scale Estimation for 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

A 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction inherits arbitrary units from structure-from-motion, so it cannot be measured or used to place AR content until someone supplies a scale. Both standard recipes are fragile on real captures: on five indoor scenes with tape-measured ceilings, a quantile floor-to-ceiling heuristic errs by 27% and metric-depth alignment by 35-71% across three networks. We present METRIC-GS, a selective scale estimator for real indoor photographs: it reads the scale off the reconstruction's own semantic content and, just as importantly, refuses when the evidence is thin. A frozen 2D teacher labels Gaussians by visibility-aware voting; instance-level size priors for architectural and furniture classes (doors, tables, the floor-to-ceiling gap) are fused by a robust maximum-likelihood step; and an inlier-mass test turns thin evidence into an explicit abstention that doubles as a router to a heavyweight metric model. MASt3R remains the more accurate standalone estimator (9.9%), but routing only our refusals to it matches running it everywhere with 60% fewer invocations; this selective hybrid is the configuration we advocate. Visibility-aware voting raises the scenes the estimator can act on from 16 to 28 at unchanged deployed accuracy. Applied unchanged to 13 phone and drone captures of our own, on which MASt3R fails its own registration gate 12 times, the system improves all five captures it acts on and degrades none. We also report that a current feed-forward metric model scores 2.3% on a benchmark inside its training mixture and 28-35% on captures it has never seen: a published metric-scale number is a best case, not a deployment estimate.

<h3 id="paper-20">20. GaussianRenderFormer: Transformer-based Rendering of 3D Gaussian Splats</h3>

Experiments on 5 benchmarks demonstrate that SplatQ outperforms state-of-the-art pruning methods in aggregate reconstruction quality, spatial quality balance, and rendering speed. Ablations confirm that budget smoothing improves local detail reconstruction, while interaction modeling improves Gaussian selection quality under a fixed budget.

6D pose estimation of novel objects without CAD models remains an open challenge: model-free methods must build the object representation from scratch during onboarding and have long trailed their model-based counterparts. Our refiner optimizes the object-to-camera pose with combined photometric and geometric losses under a continuous rotation parametrization free of gradient singularities. <h3 id="paper-96">96. Measurable Semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting with Joint Region and Depth Supervision for Interactive Building Inspection</h3>

Image-based 3D reconstruction provides a scalable representation for building inspection and maintenance. <h3 id="paper-97">97. When pose graphs are not enough: a failure analysis of pose-graph-based camera estimation for Gaussian splatting</h3>

Exterior Gaussian-splat twins for real-estate listings are captured to a fixed recipe: a consumer drone orbits a single property in several overlapping passes at stepped altitudes, with occasional vertical ascents, so the imagery is dense, sequential, GPS- and time-tagged, and revisits each facade from many angles. This structure is tantalizing: it suggests camera poses could be recovered by a dense pose-graph optimization (PGO) whose only unknowns are the poses, far fewer than the joint pose-and-point problem bundle adjustment (BA) solves. PGO is mature in robotics, but almost always as post-processing: it redistributes the drift of an already-decent trajectory once loop closures tie it together. Splatting captures push that regime to its limit, every frame closing loops with dozens of others, raising a clean question: does pose-only optimization reach BA accuracy? <h3 id="paper-98">98. 2DGS-Planner: Rasterization-based Path Planning in 2D Gaussian Splatting Map</h3>

Gaussian splatting provides an explicit and efficiently rasterizable scene representation for robot navigation. However, individual Gaussian primitives may not reliably represent obstacles as they are jointly optimized through alpha-composited rendering from a finite set of reconstruction views. We propose 2DGS-Planner, a path planner for ground robots that reads planning-relevant geometry from a 2D Gaussian splatting (2DGS) map through rasterization, rather than treating individual Gaussian primitives as obstacles. During offline roadmap construction, multi-view attribution converts rendered normal dispersion into structural scores for non-ground disks supported by the reconstruction views. These scores guide adaptive node sampling on the ground. Path-aligned orthographic queries validate candidate edges, while cylindrical queries estimate local clearance fields that are cached on the edges. During online planning, graph search initializes a route, and path refinement reuses the cached fields while accounting for the robot's dimensions and ground constraints. Experiments demonstrate improved roadmap connectivity, more accurate clearance estimation, and higher planning success compared with the tested baselines. These results support rasterization as an effective geometric query interface for planning directly on Gaussian maps. Code and data are available at https://2dgs-planner.github.io/

<h3 id="paper-99">99. PAM-ToD: Plug-and-Play Appearance Modeling for Cross-Time-of-Day 3D Gaussian Splatting</h3>

Adapting a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) road scene to a new time of day requires learning appearance changes from a few anchor images while preserving consistent, real-time rendering. We propose PAM-ToD, a lightweight plug-in that learns color corrections while keeping the pre-trained 3DGS parameters fixed. PAM-ToD scales each Gaussian's existing color to model illumination changes and uses an additive term for additional brightness, such as when street lamps turn on at night. Under a simplified image formation model, unchanged surface albedo can be eliminated from the relation between source and target appearances, allowing us to learn these corrections without separately estimating albedo and illumination. The model corrects colors across the scene while allowing the corrections to vary by location and by Gaussian. To guide learning from a few anchor images, it discourages abrupt spatial changes in these corrections. We also introduce CARLA-ToD, a benchmark with matching geometry, camera poses, and moving-object trajectories across three times of day. A few target-time anchor images are used to train each plug-in, while separate views are used for evaluation. Across the static and dynamic settings, PAM-ToD achieves higher PSNR and lower LPIPS than the baselines, even when the anchor images come from a single synchronized capture across multiple cameras.

<h3 id="paper-100">100. FlyMark: Training-Free Invisible Watermarking of 3D Gaussian Splatting via a Fruit Fly Connectome</h3>

A trained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scene ships as a portable parameter array that can be copied, pruned, requantized, or repackaged outside its training pipeline, so ownership evidence is most useful when it lives in the released parameters and remains checkable long after the embedding tooling is gone. Existing 3DGS watermarks typically tie embedding or extraction to scene optimization, a learned decoder, or rendered views, so the evidence survives only as long as a second trained artifact does. FlyMark instead writes a keyed message into the parameters a 3DGS file already stores. Its carrier directions are derived from the photoreceptors of a published connectome, a citable versioned artifact that fixes the geometry exhaustively and leaves nothing to tune per scene. A virtual observer reads cone-wise apparent luminance along a scene-normalized orbit from stored centers, colors, and opacities; a keyed dithered quantization-index-modulation code replicates each message bit across these observations; and one sparse bounded least-squares solve realizes the targets through achromatic shifts of existing degree-zero colors under a hard per-channel linear-RGB bound. All geometry and higher-order appearance parameters are preserved bit-identically, and extraction needs only cone queries, rounding, and majority voting. Under a model-domain threat model on synthetic and real scenes, FlyMark attains high clean bit accuracy and visual fidelity while cleanly separating matched from wrong keys.
